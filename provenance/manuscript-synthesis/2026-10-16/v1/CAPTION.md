**Figure. Spectral differences support recognition, but quantity remains constrained by library variability and ambiguity.** (A) Corrected NEON category medians and pointwise interquartile ranges across polygon/year observations show structured, overlapping spectra in stored units; absolute reflectance scaling remains unresolved. Grey gaps mark excluded atmospheric regions; upper bars show synthetic OLI centers/full widths at half maximum. (B) Effects (first category minus second) are cohort medians across four observed site/year groups. Faint cells lack consistent corrected support and direction in all four; they are not zero effects. All ten whole-spectrum contrasts are supported. (C) Recognition accumulates across wavelengths. Selected and random subsets use matched nested validation; solid/open circles denote new polygons and dashed/filled squares held sites. Envelopes are observed fold/seed ranges, not confidence intervals; Full uses all 360 coordinates. (D) Held-site confusion (rounded row percentages; true rows, predicted columns) and aligned recall shifts reveal uneven transfer, especially Woody shrub (46.3%). (E) Held-site mean calibration shows false abundance at zero and high-fraction shrinkage. Category ordering/colors link recognition and quantification without a population correlation claim. (F) Ridge120 lowers mean-fold RMSE versus MESMA15 by 39.8%/25.6% for new-parent/held-site targets. Using the same 15 MESMA parents removes that mean NEON advantage: Ridge15 0.365/0.387, MESMA15 0.359/0.362. Thin ranges span observed folds. Retained diversity/training bundles also change; intrinsic algorithm superiority is not established. Error can decline in highly ambiguous targets while alternative near-fitting compositions remain feasible; moderate ambiguity can favor MESMA. All abundance fractions shown here are controlled synthetic algebraic fractions; independent field fractional-cover validation remains outstanding.
