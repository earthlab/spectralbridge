# Primary definitions and implementation decisions

Roberts et al. (1998), https://doi.org/10.1016/S0034-4257(98)00037-6 : MESMA varies spectral candidates and model size per observation and evaluates physical fractions plus residual criteria. Their numerical thresholds are study-specific; importing 0.025 as a universal pass threshold is unjustified. More components can increase competing admissible models rather than improve material discrimination.

Dennison & Roberts (2003), https://doi.org/10.1016/S0034-4257(03)00135-4 ; author preprint https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~pdennison/pubs/rse_dennison2003a_preprint.pdf : EAR averages each candidate's modelling RMSE over other same-class spectra, excluding itself. Here the explicit two-endmember model is candidate plus zero photometric shade, with non-shade fraction constrained [0,1]. This stricter fraction convention is disclosed; no hidden brightness normalization.

Roth, Dennison & Roberts (2012), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2012.08.030 ; https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~pdennison/reprints/denn/2012_Roth_etal_RSE.pdf : MASA ranks same-class mean angles; CoB greedily covers previously unmodelled same-class training spectra using an RMSE threshold, breaking ties by EAR. IES starts with the best training-kappa pair and adds/removes candidates while kappa improves. It need not retain rare classes. Size limits/candidate budgets and class-completion hybrids are named adaptations, not exact reproductions of every paper setting. The source's equations and sections 2.4, 3.4 were checked before implementation.

Schaaf et al. (2011), https://doi.org/10.2747/1548-1603.48.3.324 ; author version https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~pdennison/reprints/denn/2011_Schaaf_etal_GISRS.pdf supports the kappa-based IES add/remove definition; this training selection objective must be evaluated on separately held-out identities.

Somers, Asner, Tits & Coppin (2011), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2011.03.003 ; author/institutional record https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/endmember-variability-in-spectral-mixture-analysis-a-review/ : endmember variability includes spatial/temporal/material effects; multiple candidate spectra, weighting/feature choices and physical models address different parts of that problem. An empirical Monte Carlo bundle is variability sensitivity, not automatic uncertainty calibration.

FCLS is nonnegative least squares on the sum-one simplex. The implementation will independently verify small-model exact active-face projection against constrained numerical optimization. L1 on that simplex is constant: it cannot be represented as a meaningful sparse benchmark without changing the model or penalty. Spectral angle is shape-only and must accompany magnitude/fraction errors. Absolute reflectance thresholds remain blocked by the merged library's missing per-source unit headers.
